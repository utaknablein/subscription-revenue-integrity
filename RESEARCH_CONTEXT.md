# Research context and evidentiary boundaries

The prototype separates three hypotheses for subscription revenue durability:

1. **Customer value:** usage and outcome indicators may help explain renewal durability, but infrequent use is not proof of unwanted billing.
2. **Economic quality:** full-price renewals, incentive dependence and contribution reveal different aspects of subscription economics; none individually establishes causal customer value.
3. **Choice integrity:** awareness should be measured through explicit research or validated observations; cancellation friction through actual user journeys and appropriate completion metrics. Never infer intention solely from recurring payments.

## Foundational reading

- Einav, Klopack and Mahoney, *Selling Subscriptions*, American Economic Review (2025). https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20231612
- Miller, Sahni and Strulov-Shlain, work on consumer inertia and automatic renewal (2026). Verify the most recent published version before quoting numerical conclusions.
- Harvard Business Review, *Should Your Subscription Business Use Auto-Renew?* (2026). https://hbr.org/2026/05/should-your-subscription-business-use-auto-renew

These authors are not affiliated with or endorsing this prototype. The framework is an original proposed synthesis, not a claimed scientific discovery.

## Validation requirements

- Test whether the three measures predict out-of-sample contribution durability using longitudinal data.
- Measure survey sampling and response bias, churn censoring and renewal timing.
- Preserve legally required transparency and accessible cancellation for all trial arms.
- Use randomized assignment or credible causal designs for claims about incentive effectiveness.
- Disclose sample uncertainty, possible confounding and relevant disparate effects.
